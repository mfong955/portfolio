The Problem I Was Solving

Companies, politicians, and algorithms compete for our attention and shape how we think. I kept noticing a pattern: people were operating from entirely different frames. I wanted a way to stress-test my own understanding and genuinely engage with opposing views rather than just dismissing them.

What I Built

Unbias is a suite of eight AI-powered tools designed to promote clearer civic thinking. Each tool targets a specific failure mode in how people engage with news and policy:

  • Ask Anything: Question news and current events without agenda or bias baked into the framing
  • AIs Debate: Watch economists and social impact analysts debate a topic from multiple angles, exposing tradeoffs I might have missed
  • Opposing View: Receive the strongest possible counter-argument to my own position—steel-manning, not straw-manning
  • Decode News: Analyze articles to separate facts from spin and surface what's being omitted
  • What If...?: Model policy consequences and trace historical precedents for a proposed course of action
  • Find Common Ground: Identify shared values across seemingly opposed viewpoints
  • Voter Guide: Understand ballot measures and campaign messaging in plain language
  • Content Studio: Convert analysis into formats for broader sharing

What I Learned from Using It

I built this primarily for my own use, and it changed how I read the news. A few specific patterns emerged:

  • Perspective gaps are structural, not factual: Most disagreements I encountered weren't about different facts—they were about different values hierarchies. The "Opposing View" tool made this viscerally clear.
  • Omissions matter as much as spin: "Decode News" routinely surfaced what wasn't said—context and counterexamples that changed the framing of an article entirely.
  • AI transparency builds trust: Showing reasoning alongside every response—not just the conclusion—made the tool genuinely useful rather than just another authoritative voice.

Design Principle

Every response shows its reasoning. The goal isn't to tell users what to think—it's to make the thinking process visible so users can disagree with it, challenge it, and sharpen their own conclusions. The platform is explicitly non-partisan: it presents tradeoffs, not verdicts.
